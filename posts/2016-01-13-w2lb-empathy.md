For some time this semester, I distanced myself from activities that fulfilled some purpose for me. Or, at least made me feel like I wasted less of my life on irrelevant tasks. Fortunately or unfortunately (can't say I've decided, yet), some events transpired over the past few weeks that provided me with new perspective. Something I'd like to share with you, the reader.

When I first started the Words to Live By Series (1) (2) (third deleted) it lacked purpose, but I felt compelled to chronicle something. Whether it be mindset, personal development, news, technology improvements, or perhaps a combination of the former or latter... That's how my adventure on the Odyssey began. I'd like to get back to that mini-series, if just for a moment.

This one revolves around empathy. That thing where you identify with a psychological event vicariously. It's compassion, pity, appreciation for your companion. It's intuitive. But, it's also something you can work on intellectually.

Yes, there's the more basic affective empathy -- the physical emulation of another's feelings. The way they react to something. Or, perhaps your reaction to a state you see someone in.

Cognitive empathy is the other side, it's your capacity to understand the emotions of others. That's the worthy development pursuit.

Regardless of your belief on how you got here there's an inherent amount of luck involved with your procurement. It could be something along the lines of a product of a million/billion year old machine that refines its process each iteration, or you could be hand selected by an intelligent designer, or something else. Honestly, I don't know which seems more incalculable.

At that scope, are you fully responsible for your successes and failures? Are you comfortable judging others or acting on others for their successes and failures which they are likely not in full control over?

I think there's an easy argument here about the futility of life, in which nihilists would rejoice.

But, as many of us have found out, a lot of things become meaningless if you look too far out. Does one homework assignment matter when you compare it to your whole life? No, but it does affect your immediate circumstance.

That scope -- that change of timelines and the way it impacts the events around you, should humble you.

It should point you to the idea that no one conversation, action, event, idea, are damning, irrecoverable, or irreproducible. It shows that things don't require such high stakes, nor ultimate conclusions.

That idea is enough to be a better lover, companion, and friend. It's enough to not feel rushed, or add stress to the lives of others. It's enough to become the adult in the room. It is enough to power a radiantly calm temperament, zealous, generous, and productive ethic, all foundationally fortified by a patient resolve.

There are many ways in which we feel defeat. Whether the political landscape drains you, the economic, ideological, cultural, or other platforms may serve to erode. Indeed, looking at how large problems are and others will make things seem impractical to approach.

But, with some work, empathy is what will take you out of the nihilistic shell.

That persona proselytized some of the most prominent and prolific phenomena the world observed. Civil rights movements. Economic reform. Political treaties.

You're the next step. Just requires you to burden yourself with the work for the sake of the people around you.
