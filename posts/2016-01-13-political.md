In the news over the past few weeks there's been a lot of media coverage regarding the government and legislation towards transgender people. If you haven't heard of North Carolina's controversial House Bill No. 2, the state government passed a bill forcing students who identify as a different gender than the gender of birth to use the bathroom corresponding to their birth gender. This sparked a whole debate over gender equality, and even the White House has gotten involved, providing guidance on legislation for all school districts.

The U.S. federal government is attempting to take a completely non-discriminatory stance on the issue, which is inherently creating bias. In the fed's attempt to create equality, the social climate is worsening. Political correctness (PC) is the active avoidance of expression that is used to defame a socially disadvantaged or marginalized group. In the times of old, this was called "common courtesy." Although a courtesy, not required from a person to be a member of society.

Political correctness is an inclusionary state of mind, meaning, the ideology seeks to incorporate and include people of all kinds. The thought process wants equality for all types of people, whether in the courtrooms or bathrooms. It self-describes as political theory for "open-minded" citizens. Political correctness aims to step on the toes of nobody, aims to dishearten or inconvenience nobody, and aims to exclude nobody in the process.

Is it not hypocritical that a group attempting to include all refuses to accept the people who aren't as openminded? They'll accept people from all origins, histories, and walks of life, but if they share a different political ideology that isn't allowed.

I've seen my fair share of protests and demonstrations in Boulder. From my experience, I have yet to witness one not contain two groups yelling at each other with statements like "close-minded," "ignorant," or "uneducated." There is nothing wrong with using these statements. Just how they are normally used by millennials is an issue.

Political correctness will be the death of debate, rhetoric, and argument. Not being able to survive in an ecosystem with peers that challenge, rebuke, or question your thought process does not forge stronger rhetoricians. Not being able to have an unemotional conversation with someone who fundamentally disagrees with you serves no purpose in life. Not being able to accept someone's point of view as different and equally deserving of merit as yours is a disservice to society.

Nobody profits when you think only your point of view is correct. So, for those of you who think of yourself as having a more open-minded view of the world, continue. But, don't cut out the people around you who don't share the same ideas. Chances are, neither of you are right, and the best solution is somewhere in the middle.

For some reason, moderate thought is dying out. As I mentioned last week, our generation moves forward to fight the proliferation of radicalism. Be part of the solution, because it's a solution we desperately need.
