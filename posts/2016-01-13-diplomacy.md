The people of Turkey continue to rush the streets for days on end nationwide. This revolt is obviously about more than just a park. Days ago, citizens organized a peaceful protest in Gezi park to demonstrate to the government the people did not support their plans to demolish the park and erect a shopping mall. The mall would be a nod to historic Ottoman Empire barracks, an increasingly worrying symbol to the people as Erdogan's government attempts to make Turkey a non-secular society once more.

Before Erdogan's AKP party, Turkey was a nation of secularism. Allowing women to not wear headscarves (even outlawing them, for a short time, in some locations), empowering a society not afraid of alcohol or sexuality, and promoting free expression were benefits to the people. However, when political movements came into such conflict with traditional Islam, the military would stage a coup and takeover the country.

The AKP party did give way to a time of unprecedented economic stability, though. Although they would pass laws to marginalize the anti-AKP and anti-Islamists, their opposition was rarely heard as the political changes didn't affect many people (being a 98 percent Muslim country).

That all changed with the tear gas. This ignited the Turkish people to revolt. Nationwide media blackouts were seen on Friday and Saturday. It's tough to know exactly what's happening there because of this, and the journalists being arrested. Some clips show extreme resolve of protesters to remain non-violent in the face of water cannons and some show burning cars and vandalism.

Erdogan fired back. Although he's not supposed to be in power still, he is using auxiliary powers to enact his rule. Tens of thousands of teachers fired, all Turkish students banned from traveling abroad, thousands of federal teaching licenses revoked, governors fired, thousands of judges dismissed, demand for all deans of universities to resign... these are the underlings of creating a society only acceptable to an autocrat.

In one of my previous articles, I wrote about how our generation moves forward to fight hyperinflation and the proliferation of radicalism. It's true. There isn't a need for a society ruled by one perspective. However, there also isn't a need for America to always be the enforcer.

It is our place to do what we can as citizens, whether that be financial support of emigrants from Turkey to the E.U. or further abroad. If you feel compelled to help, there are many non-profits that make a livelihood from doing so. If you feel this isn't your struggle to support, then be grateful that your government supports your freedom of expression.

There's no question that Erdogan shouldn't be in power, and shouldn't be using his power as an end to his own means. Turkey will not be the tipping point for the destabilization of the entire Middle East. Our job is to do what we can, not do everything for them.
